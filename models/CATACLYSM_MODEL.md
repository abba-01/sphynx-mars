# Coupled-Cataclysm Stress Model

**Question tested.** Is there a single event — a great impact or bombardment — that
caused, in sequence: ground-ice vaporization → a transient atmosphere → crustal
fracturing → confined-aquifer release → outflow floods / a northern sea, with the
moon story folded in? This document reports whether existing science addresses that
question, builds the coupled model, and stresses it to failure.

**Status of the science (short answer).** Every *link* is published, separately. The
*coupling as one event* is not — it has never been assembled as a single falsifiable
model, and when you do assemble it, it fails a test the sub-fields don't run because
they each work in their own epoch. That failure is the useful result.

## What already exists (each module is real)

| Module | Established science |
|---|---|
| Impact → transient atmosphere & rainfall | Segura et al. 2002 (*Science* 298, 1977) and 2008 (*JGR* 113, E11007): basin-scale impacts drive steam atmospheres and rainfall lasting years to centuries (~2.6 m/yr deluge). Confirmed by the search-rendered abstracts. |
| Impact → crustal fracture / megaregolith | Standard impact cratering (Melosh 1989); km-scale fractured megaregolith observed on the Moon/Mars. |
| Confined-aquifer breakout → outflow floods | Carr 1979 (superlithostatic head); Clifford 1993 (global cryosphere/hydrosphere). Search rendering (Wikipedia/ESA) states outflow channels form "along zones of superlithostatic pressure generated by pronounced elevation differences" — the head mechanism, published. |
| Northern ocean / shorelines / tsunami | Parker et al. 1989/1993 (shorelines); Head et al. 1999 (Deuteronilus contact); Rodriguez et al. 2016, 2022 (two mega-tsunami deposits, Chryse). |
| Crustal dichotomy = giant impact (Borealis) | Marinova, Aharonson & Asphaug 2008; Andrews-Hanna, Zuber & Banerdt 2008 (*Nature* 453). |
| Obliquity governed by a moon | Laskar & Robutel 1993; Laskar et al. 2004: Earth's Moon stabilizes tilt; Mars' tilt is chaotic (0–60°). |
| Mars' moons from an impact disk | Rosenblatt et al. 2016; Canup & Salmon 2018. |

**Gap:** nobody couples these into one event and dates the whole chain against itself.
That is what the model below does.

## The model

`models/cataclysm_stress.py` — order-of-magnitude physics with explicit Mars
constants and Monte Carlo uncertainty (N = 200,000, seeded). Four tests. Run it:
`python3 models/cataclysm_stress.py`. It is a **consistency calculator, not a
measurement**; every constant is sourced in-code.

### Test A — Transient atmosphere: PLAUSIBLE, but only as pulses
An impactor vaporizes ground ice; added global surface pressure is compared to the
611 Pa liquid-water floor. Result: impactors above **~31 km** (at 3 % energy-to-vapor
efficiency) clear the floor globally; 95 % of the sampled 20–250 km range does.
**But the water rains out in years–centuries** (Segura). So an impact buys a
*transient* warm/wet window, not a standing atmosphere — and on a planet Mars' size,
what condenses isn't fully retained. Atmospheres here are pulses.

### Test B — Aquifer breakout: the coupling the model AFFIRMS
Head overpressure from a 1–5 km Tharsis→Chryse gradient is ~**11 MPa** (median). That
alone can breach a seal only ~**1 km** thick — and against a realistic 2–6 km early
cryosphere, **head alone breaches in 0 % of sampled cases**. Add impact-fracturing
(30–70 % effective seal weakening) and **13 %** break through. So the
impact→fracture→flood link is physically load-bearing: the impact is what tips a
marginal hydraulic system over. This is the one link the model actively supports.

### Test C — Obliquity: a moon governs, and Mars is the inverse case
Spin-axis torque proxy (mass/distance³): **Phobos contributes ~13× less** than the Sun
(Deimos ~1500× less; both combined ~13× less), whereas **Earth's Moon is ~2× the Sun's**
on Earth — a ~28× contrast in the moon-to-Sun ratio between the two planets. That asymmetry is why Earth's
tilt is pinned near 23.5° and Mars' wanders chaotically 0–60°, sloshing ice
pole↔equator. A big moon *does* quietly govern climate — but Mars never had one, and
Phobos is spiraling *in* toward destruction in ~30–50 Myr. "The moon takes over" is
real physics running backwards here: nothing ever took over, which is Mars' problem.

### Test D — Chronology: the decisive falsification
The grand "one event" claim predicts the phenomena share an age. Fed the published
ages (with deliberately *inflated* uncertainties, to steelman the claim):

| Event | Age (Ga) | ±1σ |
|---|---|---|
| Borealis basin / dichotomy | 4.50 | 0.10 |
| Core dynamo shutdown | 4.10 | 0.15 |
| Late Heavy Bombardment peak | 3.95 | 0.10 |
| Ares Vallis outflow floods | 3.40 | 0.30 |
| Northern ocean / tsunami | 3.40 | 0.20 |
| Phobos/Deimos (impact-disk) | 4.30 | 0.40 |

A single common epoch gives best-fit t₀ = 4.10 Ga with **χ² = 36.2 on 5 dof
(reduced 7.2), p ≈ 1.7×10⁻⁶** — the single-event hypothesis is **rejected
decisively**. The events instead resolve into two clusters ~1 Gyr apart: an early one
(dichotomy / dynamo / LHB / moons, ~4.2 Ga, but itself loose — reduced χ² ≈ 5, i.e.
also spread, not a single instant) and a **late one that is genuinely tight**: floods +
ocean + tsunami at **3.40 Ga, χ² = 0.0** — statistically coincident.

## What the model concludes (honest reading)

1. **The grand single-cataclysm story is false** at p < 10⁻⁶. The processes are real
   but span ~1.5 billion years; no one event did them all.
2. **A short coupled chain is real and recurs:** impact → transient atmosphere →
   fracture-assisted aquifer breakout → local flood is viable *per event* (Test B),
   and the flood/ocean/tsunami trio at 3.4 Ga is a tight, genuinely coeval cluster —
   the strongest thing the model finds, and exactly the terrain the Pathfinder Twin
   Peaks sit in ("flood debris," per the NASA caption).
3. **The framing that survives** is not "one catastrophe" but "a small viable chain
   that fired repeatedly, inside a planet too small to keep what each pulse created" —
   the atmosphere condensed, the dynamo died, the moon that would have steadied it
   never formed.

This is how a dramatic hypothesis is supposed to end when it's tested: not dismissed,
but *resolved* — the true parts kept with their real ages, the overreach (one event,
one story) killed by a number.

## How this was stressed (and how to stress it further)

- **Monte Carlo** over impactor size/velocity/efficiency, head, cryosphere thickness,
  and seal weakening — results are reported as bands, not point values.
- **Steelmanning**: Test D used inflated age uncertainties; the single-epoch model
  fails anyway.
- **Sign checks**: the "crust rises from saturation" intuition was corrected — pore-
  pressure uplift is centimetric; the large vertical motions are ocean-load subsidence
  and rebound (the shoreline-deformation literature's actual signal).
- **Open stress not yet run** (honest limits): a proper χ² needs primary age
  distributions, not search-rendered central values (fetch the dating papers per
  `data/SOURCING_LIST.md` before treating Test D as more than indicative); the impact
  energy→vapor efficiency (1–6 %) is the softest parameter and deserves a coupled
  thermal model; Test C's torque proxy is not a full secular-resonance calculation
  (Laskar's is). These are the next escalations if the question is pursued.

## Scope note

This is an **exploratory hypothesis-stress exercise**, not part of the repository's
validated image-measurement core. It is labeled as such deliberately: it exists to
apply the same discipline (name the null, put a number on it, publish the negative
result) to a planetary-evolution idea raised in discussion. No claim here should be
cited as established; the citations above should be verified at primary source before
any of this leaves working notes.
