## What Pareidolia Actually Requires

### **Complexity Threshold for Pareidolia**

**Definition:** Pareidolia is the tendency to perceive meaningful patterns (faces, objects, familiar forms) in **random or ambiguous stimuli**.

**Key Requirements:**
1. **Ambiguous stimulus** - The pattern must be unclear or random
2. **Complex interpretation** - The brain must impose a specific, meaningful identity
3. **Subjective assignment** - Different people see different things
4. **No objective grounding** - The "meaning" exists only in perception, not measurement

### **Examples of True Pareidolia:**
- **Face in clouds:** Random cloud formations interpreted as human faces
- **Man in the Moon:** Lunar surface features seen as facial features  
- **Virgin Mary in toast:** Random burn patterns interpreted as religious imagery
- **Animal shapes in rocks:** Geological formations seen as familiar creatures
